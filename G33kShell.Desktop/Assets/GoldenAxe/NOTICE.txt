Golden Axe private reference data

Source: https://github.com/atomic14/golden-axe
Reference revision: f669472505444939e11c66bb0d33a7469e282787
Target: Golden Axe (1989), arcade System 16B, MAME goldnaxe set 6 (US).
Original game, artwork and data: Sega. They are not covered by the source-code header.

intro.png is the original FBI opening card captured from the US set-6
arcade cold boot in MAME. title.png is the original arcade title still.
title-layers.png contains the scrolling cloud texture, gold emblem and footer
reconstructed from the US set-6 title observations. The moving
letters, settled wordmark and red splat use the original sprite atlas.
The parchment art, title and opening images are separate from the source-code license.

The local files were copied from the reference's public/assets directory:
  sprites.json, sprites-0.png, tiles.png, magic.json
  areas/0.json -> level1.json
  areas/1.json -> level2.json (Turtle Village)
  areas/2.json -> level3.json (third main area, arcade stage 5)
  areas/3.json -> level4.json (fourth main area, arcade stage 7)
  areas/4.json -> level5.json (final main area, arcade stage 9)

The first four area files also supply the camp spawn lists and night palettes.

forest-palettes.json contains eight Stage 1 scenery palette snapshots from
MAME US set 6. Keys are camera progress values (4417 through 4479). Each value
stores groups 64 through 92 as little-endian 32-bit RGB words, matching the
format of the level palettes. The first position triggers a 126-tick blend
through the samples, before the separate boss sunset.

The eagle area's drifting feathers use Dean-identified original atlas frame 1042.
The half-size rendering and muted palette 0x87 approximate the MAME reference;
exact hardware scaling, palette selection and drift timing remain unverified.

On 24 November 2026, Dean's MAME US set-6 lower-edge capture repaired 191
previously uncaptured map cells: 4 in level2.json, 49 in level3.json, and 138 in
level5.json. Of these, 174 reuse existing atlas patterns. The remaining 17 use
nine original tile patterns appended as atlas IDs 8064-8072 (the final atlas row).
Palette groups and priority bits come from the captured arcade tilemap words.
All previous atlas pixels and previously captured map cells are unchanged.
Reference: Brain bf201b5ac180d4cf, golden-axe-bottom-tile-handoff.zip,
missing-cells.csv and nine-missing-atlas-tiles.png, captured 23 November 2026.
These repairs cover five sampled scenes, not every lower edge or camera position.
The camera paths also include the measured lower limits at Stage 5 camera X -666
(bucket -84, minimum Y -749) and Stage 9 X -632 (bucket -79, minimum Y -736).
Other recorded camera bounds and all height/collision data remain unchanged.

The loader fills remaining uncaptured background cells using the nearest captured tile.
This hides gaps at camera edges; it is a visual approximation, not recovered ROM
data. This runtime fallback does not overwrite the recovered source JSON cells.

On 25 November 2026, Dean's MAME US set-6 sky handoff supplied a complete repeating
64x32 hardware tile map for Stage 7. All 6,477 sampled cells across pages 8-11 agree
at the same local coordinates; their union covers all 2,048 cells. The 120 ROM
tile codes 15488-15607 were matched bijectively to the existing atlas through
shared multi-row tile arrangements. level4.json's separate sky layer reuses those
unchanged original patterns, palette groups and priority bits; no art was generated.
Its scrollX samples come from the push trace at ticks 4627-4817 and the long trace
at offsets 191-193. Continuing the final 6px/tick rate and -1.5px/tick vertical
offset matches all 642 long-trace samples modulo the repeated hardware pages.
References: Brain 85b1ee0e0a59e8f5, stage7-sky-hardware-tiles.csv and
stage7-sky-registers.csv. Initial activation and behavior beyond the capture are
approximations. Non-sky scenery and camp backgrounds retain their original layers.
The far-right eagle foreground contains 130 restored cells from the same
US set-6 tile RAM captures. Three traces agree on those tile words, and the
mapping matches 255 neighboring cells and 45 directly visible restored cells.
The restored cells retain the arcade palette groups and priority bits, and all
of their pen patterns already exist in the tile atlas.
The Stage 7 camera audit also exposed an uncaptured underside beginning at world
(896, 376). Restored 153 foreground cells in rows 376–416 from the US set-6 tile
trace. The capture matches all 144 sampled cells already present in that region.
Three missing pen patterns were appended to tiles.png as atlas IDs 8192–8194.

These extracted JSON/PNG assets are bundled with the repository and copied into
build and publish output. No upstream TypeScript source is included in the
application. Native code uses the documented extracted format.

The original Sega assets remain separate from this repository's source-code license.
There is no runtime download or browser dependency.

Tyris's 7-9 potion spells are reconstructed at runtime in TyrisMagic.cs using the
original bundled artwork. Sprite families, palettes and visibility windows follow
Dean's MAME US set 6 timing handoff (Brain 24e624f855650691); settled dragon piece
positions were matched against tyris-max-magic.png. Movement, high-count potion
fan spacing and meteor trajectories are approximations, not captured frame replays.
Flame lighting and smoke are modern presentation effects.

Stage 7 static scenery alignment: level4.json uses 1:1 vertical background scrolling
to align the captured pavement with the foreground house base. The extracted fitted
factor (0.993, offset 1) exposed a one-pixel backing-color seam as the camera moved.
The separately animated eagle sky retains its independent scrolling.
The underside below the Stage 7 bridge uses the US set-6 foreground words
at X 664–888, Y 400–424. Its tile patterns come from the original tile ROM.

The Stage 9 rescue uses the original captive and chain sprites, with poses and
positions from Dean's US set-6 hardware trace (Brain 9ab84e0b46608736).
story-map1.png through story-map4.png contain the original parchments
and ordered route ink tiles from native US set-6 MAME captures.
Their JSON timelines preserve the visible arcade text and per-tick quill
positions. tools/build-golden-axe-story-map.py extracts the sheets and
timelines from the sprite-free captures and hardware quill positions.
ArcadeParchment draws all five map scenes with the original tile font
and feather sprite 1042 using palette 0x80.

victory-map.png contains the US set-6 parchment and 21 cursive Fin
ink tiles extracted from a native-size MAME capture with sprites hidden.
tools/build-golden-axe-victory-map.py extracts this sheet from the capture.
The victory feather follows cubic Bézier segments through the ROM tile
positions at 0x7788, synced to the original ink reveals.
